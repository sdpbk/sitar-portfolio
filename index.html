About

A sitar player trained in the gayaki ang tradition of the Imdadkhani gharana, one of the oldest and most influential lineages in Hindustani classical music. This style — pioneered by Ustad Vilayat Khan — transforms the sitar into a singing instrument, emulating the fluid phrasing, meend, and murki of khayal vocalism through refined left-hand technique and melodic continuity.

Drawing primarily from the Vilayatkhani approach and further informed by the playing of Ustad Shahid Parvez Khan — the seventh-generation torchbearer of the Etawah gharana — the focus is on lyrical raga exposition, from meditative alaap to intricate taan patterns rooted in vocal idioms.

Based in New York City, drawing connections between the microtonal expressiveness of Hindustani music and the harmonic traditions of Western classical music and opera.

Lineage & Training

  • Imdadkhani Gharana — Gayaki Ang

    Trained in the sitar tradition of the Imdadkhani (Etawah) gharana, a lineage tracing back through Ustad Imdad Khan, Ustad Enayat Khan, and Ustad Vilayat Khan. The gharana's hallmark is the gayaki ang — a revolutionary approach in which the sitar mirrors the expressive contours of khayal vocalism through controlled string bending, sustained melodic phrasing, and intricate ornamental patterns that give the instrument a singing quality.

  • Vilayatkhani Tradition

    Primary stylistic foundation in the approach of Ustad Vilayat Khan, widely regarded as the architect of modern sitar. Vilayat Khan introduced a left-hand technique that achieved unprecedented melodic continuity on a plucked instrument — lyrical alap inspired by the merukhand system of the Kirana gharana, khayal-ang murkis, and taan patterns drawn from diverse vocal traditions including those of Ustad Abdul Karim Khan and Ustad Faiyaz Khan.

  • Shahid Parvez Khan — Contemporary Influence

    Further informed by the playing of Ustad Shahid Parvez Khan, the seventh-generation representative of the Etawah gharana and nephew of Vilayat Khan. Shahid Parvez Khan extends the gayaki ang tradition with distinctive picking patterns, technical virtuosity in both vilambit and drut gat, and an approach that balances tantrakari (instrumental technique) with deep emotional expression.

  • Cross-Tradition Engagement

    Active listener and student of Western classical music and opera, exploring structural parallels between Hindustani raga exposition and Western forms — the improvisatory cadenza, the architecture of sonata form, and the interplay of counterpoint.
