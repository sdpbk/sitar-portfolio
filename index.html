About

A sitar player rooted in the gayaki ang tradition of the Imdadkhani gharana — one of the oldest and most illustrious lineages in Hindustani classical music. The gharana's signature approach, perfected by Ustad Vilayat Khan, transforms the sitar into a singing instrument, bringing the fluid phrasing, meend, and expressive contours of khayal vocalism to a plucked string.

Style is drawn primarily from the Vilayatkhani tradition, and further informed by Ustad Shahid Parvez Khan, seventh-generation torchbearer of the Etawah gharana. The focus is lyrical raga exposition — from slow, meditative alaap to intricate taans shaped by the idioms of great vocalists.

Based in New York City, with an active interest in the structural parallels between North Indian classical music and the Western classical and operatic traditions.

Lineage & Background

  • Imdadkhani Gharana

    Rooted in one of the oldest lineages in Hindustani instrumental music, tracing through Ustad Imdad Khan, Ustad Enayat Khan, and Ustad Vilayat Khan. The gharana's hallmark is the gayaki ang — a revolutionary approach in which the sitar mirrors the expressive vocabulary of khayal vocalism.

  • Vilayatkhani Tradition (Primary)

    Primary stylistic foundation in the approach of Ustad Vilayat Khan, widely considered the architect of modern sitar. Vilayat Khan introduced a distinctive left-hand technique achieving unprecedented melodic continuity, lyrical alap rooted in the merukhand system, and taan patterns drawn from the great vocalists of the Kirana and Agra gharanas.

  • Ustad Shahid Parvez Khan

    Further informed by the playing of Shahid Parvez Khan — nephew of Vilayat Khan and seventh-generation representative of the Etawah gharana. Known for unique picking patterns, intricate midh and murki, and a balance of technical virtuosity with deep emotional expression.

  • Western Classical Engagement

    Active student of Western classical music and opera, exploring structural parallels between Hindustani raga exposition and Western forms — the arc of the cadenza, sonata form, and the counterpoint of voices.
